I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Professor Todd Austin. I work on secure computer architecture, especially for multi-chiplet systems.

My research covers hardware roots of trust for untrusted chiplets, defenses against microarchitectural side channels such as Hertzbleed, and formally verified secure processors. Day to day, that means architectural simulation in gem5, RTL design, and proofs in Lean.

I received my M.S. from Michigan in 2025 and my B.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Addis Ababa University, where I graduated with the second-highest GPA in the engineering faculty. In between, I worked at Africa to Silicon Valley (A2SV) as a software and ML engineer and as head of education. At Michigan, I have taught EECS 370 and EECS 573 as a graduate student instructor.

I'm looking for industry roles in computer architecture, hardware security, parallel computing, and performance engineering.
